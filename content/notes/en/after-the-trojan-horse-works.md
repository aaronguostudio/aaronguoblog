Watching Nolan’s _The Odyssey_, I kept thinking about the horse. Nolan recently [called AI a transparent Trojan horse](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/19/odyssey-director-christopher-nolan-calls-ai-an-obvious-trojan-horse/): everyone can see the Greeks inside. But the more interesting question comes after it. The plan works; Troy falls. Then Odysseus must face what victory cost—and find his way home.

AI conversations linger on the first question: Can it write, draw, code, and finish the job? This does not make AI a foretold disaster. It means capability is not the end of the story. The more effective a system becomes, the more urgent the second question: who carries the cost, and which judgments and responsibilities must stay with people?

Perhaps AI’s real test is not whether it can help us take the city, but whether, after success, we still know how to return home. Technology can complete an objective. Only people can decide whether the world it leaves behind is worth living in.
