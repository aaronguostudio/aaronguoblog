# Visual Postmortem

## What worked

- Keeping the Trojan Horse stopped at the edge made capability a completed mechanism rather than the hero.
- A moving human-scale boat and a single warm doorway made “success after the task” emotionally legible without a diagram.
- Large areas of calm sea and sky carried consequence and time without adding arrows or route lines.
- Ink blue, terracotta, graphite, ivory, and one amber light give the Notes archive a new visual rhythm while preserving premium restraint.
- Separate refined candidates made the mobile-clutter tradeoff visible before selection.

## What failed or weakened the image

- Candidate B added plants, layered shores, and a larger home structure. Each detail was attractive, but together they competed with the one-predicate contract.
- A mythic scene can drift toward cinematic poster energy quickly. Explicitly banning battle, storm, actor likeness, and heroic lighting prevented that failure.

## Reusable patterns

For reflective technology Notes, place the completed technical object at the edge of the composition and give the human consequence the open center. Use one ordinary human-scale action, one restrained warm signal, and environmental negative space instead of a drawn system path.

## Anti-patterns to add to global system

No global rule change is needed. Keep the article-specific warning that decorative landscape details can quietly turn three visual groups into five.
